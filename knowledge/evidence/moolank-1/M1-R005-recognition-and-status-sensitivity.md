## Research question

Do traditional Birth Number / Moolank 1 sources consistently support a preference for recognition, visibility or respect, and how should this be separated from confidence, self-esteem, ambition, dominance, entitlement and dependence on praise?

## Working definition

Recognition and status sensitivity describe how strongly a person notices acknowledgment, visibility, rank, respect and public evaluation, and how these signals influence motivation, confidence display or behaviour.

Healthy recognition sensitivity can involve valuing fair credit, respectful treatment and accurate acknowledgment of contribution. It does not automatically mean entitlement, superiority, praise addiction, narcissism, dominance or fragile self-worth.

## Scope rule

This record concerns **Birth Number / Moolank 1**, calculated from birth dates 1, 10, 19 or 28. Life Path 1, Destiny Number 1 and general Number 1 descriptions are comparison evidence only unless a source clearly identifies Birth Number or Moolank.

## Psychology bridge

Psychology is used only to define constructs and safe boundaries. It does **not** validate a relationship between birth number and recognition sensitivity.

### Recognition versus praise

Recognition is broader than praise. It can include accurate acknowledgment, credit, respect, visibility or confirmation that one’s contribution was noticed. Praise is an explicitly positive evaluation. A person may value fair credit without needing frequent admiration.

### Informational versus controlling praise

Self-determination research distinguishes feedback that communicates useful competence information from praise that pressures behaviour or controls approval. Informational acknowledgment may support competence, while controlling approval can shift attention toward external validation.

Relevant references:

- Deci and Ryan, **Intrinsic Motivation and Self-Determination in Human Behavior** (1985).
- Deci, Koestner and Ryan, work on extrinsic rewards and intrinsic motivation.

### Contingent self-worth

Contingent self-worth occurs when personal value rises or falls heavily with approval, comparison, success or status outcomes. This is distinct from simply appreciating recognition. The canonical trait must not imply that Moolank 1 self-worth is inherently approval-dependent.

### Status threat

Status sensitivity concerns awareness of rank, respect and relative standing. A threat response may appear when a person feels ignored, publicly diminished or denied fair credit, but status threat is contextual and should not be treated as universal.

Moolank 1 tradition may support a preference for visible contribution, respectful acknowledgment and fair credit, especially in roles involving responsibility, initiative or leadership.

- values accurate acknowledgment rather than empty praise;

### Conditional shadow expression

The shadow is not universal. It becomes more plausible when recognition needs combine with public comparison, unclear ownership, unequal credit, status competition, repeated invisibility, insecurity, recent success or fear of being replaced.

Possible patterns:

- feels overlooked when contribution is not publicly named;
- interprets disagreement as disrespect;
- competes for visibility beyond what the task requires;
- takes disproportionate credit for shared outcomes;
- withholds cooperation when acknowledgment seems absent;
- becomes resentful after another person receives praise;
- uses titles, rank or authority to protect status;
- performs visibly while neglecting less visible work;
- depends on applause to sustain effort;
- treats reduced recognition as proof of reduced worth.

## Initial construct boundaries

### Recognition sensitivity vs confidence

Confidence concerns belief in one’s ability to engage effectively. Recognition sensitivity concerns how acknowledgment and public evaluation affect motivation or self-presentation. Confidence can remain stable without praise, and recognition can matter even when confidence is high.

### Recognition sensitivity vs self-esteem

Self-esteem concerns general self-worth. Recognition sensitivity concerns response to external acknowledgment. Valuing fair credit does not prove fragile self-esteem.

### Recognition sensitivity vs ambition

Ambition concerns desired achievement or advancement. Recognition may accompany ambition, but a person can pursue difficult goals privately or seek visibility without sustained achievement effort.

### Recognition sensitivity vs dominance

Dominance concerns control, rank or influence over others. Recognition sensitivity concerns acknowledgment and standing. Seeking fair credit does not justify controlling others.

### Recognition sensitivity vs entitlement

Entitlement assumes one deserves special treatment regardless of contribution, agreement or context. Healthy recognition sensitivity asks for proportionate credit and respect without claiming automatic superiority.

### Recognition sensitivity vs praise dependence

Praise dependence means motivation or self-worth relies heavily on repeated approval. Recognition sensitivity may exist without dependence when the person continues meaningful effort despite delayed or absent praise.

### Recognition sensitivity vs leadership

Leadership concerns influence, coordination and responsibility for shared outcomes. Recognition may affect a leader’s motivation, but leadership effectiveness requires sharing credit, tolerating dissent and protecting team contribution.

## Provisional safe core

> Moolank 1 tradition may associate this number with visible contribution, respect and acknowledgment. In a balanced expression, recognition is valued as fair credit and useful feedback while motivation, cooperation and self-worth remain stable without constant praise. Under pressure—especially when contribution feels ignored or status feels reduced—the same sensitivity may become visibility competition, resentment, disproportionate credit-taking or dependence on approval. Scientific research does not establish that a birth number predicts recognition sensitivity, contingent self-worth or status behaviour.
