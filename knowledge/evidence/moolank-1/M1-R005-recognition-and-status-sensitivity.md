## Research question

Is the available evidence sufficient for canonical inclusion, and how should fair-credit sensitivity be separated from confidence, self-esteem, ambition, dominance, entitlement, praise dependence, social comparison and leadership?

## Final construct definition

Recognition and status sensitivity describe how strongly acknowledgment, fair credit, visibility, rank, respect and public evaluation are noticed, and how these signals may influence motivation, self-presentation or cooperation.

Healthy expression values accurate acknowledgment and proportionate credit while keeping motivation, cooperation and self-worth stable without constant praise. It does not mean entitlement, superiority, attention-seeking, dominance, narcissism, fragile self-esteem or dependence on approval.

## Psychology bridge

Psychology is used only to define constructs and boundaries. It does **not** validate a relationship between birth number and recognition sensitivity.

- **Recognition:** acknowledgment that contribution, effort or presence was noticed.
- **Praise:** an explicitly positive evaluation; only one form of recognition.
- **Informational acknowledgment:** feedback that communicates useful competence or contribution information.
- **Controlling praise:** approval used to pressure behaviour or make acceptance conditional.
- **Contingent self-worth:** personal value rising or falling heavily with approval, success, comparison or status.
- **Social comparison:** evaluating one’s ability, opinion or standing relative to others.
- **Status threat:** perceived loss of rank, respect, visibility or influence.

Relevant references include Deci and Ryan (1985), Deci, Koestner and Ryan on rewards and intrinsic motivation, Crocker and Wolfe (2001), and Festinger (1954).

## Final boundary decisions

### Recognition sensitivity vs confidence

Confidence concerns perceived capability. Recognition sensitivity concerns response to acknowledgment and public evaluation. Confidence can remain stable without praise, and recognition may matter even when confidence is high.

### Recognition sensitivity vs self-esteem

Self-esteem concerns general self-worth. Valuing fair credit does not prove fragile self-esteem. Making recognition the basis of personal worth would indicate contingent self-worth, which remains separate.

### Recognition sensitivity vs ambition

Ambition concerns desired achievement or advancement. Recognition can accompany ambition but does not define it. A person may pursue difficult goals privately.

### Recognition sensitivity vs dominance

Dominance concerns control, rank or influence over others. Seeking fair credit does not justify controlling people, suppressing dissent or claiming superior decision rights.

### Recognition sensitivity vs entitlement

Entitlement assumes special treatment regardless of contribution, agreement or context. Healthy recognition sensitivity asks for proportionate credit and respectful treatment, not automatic superiority.

### Recognition sensitivity vs praise dependence

Praise dependence means motivation or self-worth relies heavily on repeated approval. Recognition sensitivity may exist without dependence when meaningful effort continues despite delayed or absent praise.

### Recognition sensitivity vs social comparison

Social comparison is a process of evaluating oneself relative to others. It may trigger recognition concerns, but it is not the same trait.

### Recognition sensitivity vs leadership

Leadership concerns influence, coordination and responsibility for shared outcomes. Effective leadership requires sharing credit, recognizing invisible work, tolerating dissent and protecting team contribution.

### Recognition sensitivity vs disrespect

Absence of praise, disagreement or lower visibility does not automatically equal disrespect. A fair interpretation requires role, contribution, agreement and communication context.

## Canonical eligibility decision

M1-R005 is **eligible for cautious canonical inclusion as a secondary traditional tendency with conditional shadow**, not as a primary or universal trait.

Canonical inclusion is permitted only when all of the following remain explicit:

- direct traditional support is weak–moderate;
- wording uses “may value” or equivalent conditional language;

## Balanced expression

- values accurate acknowledgment rather than empty praise;
- appreciates proportionate credit for contribution;
- recognizes others without feeling diminished;
- separates respect from automatic agreement;
- remains motivated when recognition is delayed;
- communicates credit expectations directly;
- accepts shared ownership in collaborative work;
- contributes privately when the outcome matters;
- does not equate low visibility with low worth;
- distinguishes title or rank from actual contribution;
- celebrates another person’s success without treating it as personal loss;
- shares credit and notices invisible work.

## Conditional shadow expression

The shadow is not universal. It becomes more plausible when recognition concerns combine with unclear ownership, unequal credit, repeated invisibility, public comparison, status competition, recent success, insecurity or fear of replacement.

Possible patterns:

- feels overlooked when contribution is not publicly named;
- interprets disagreement as disrespect;
- competes for visibility beyond task needs;
- takes disproportionate credit for shared outcomes;
- withholds cooperation when acknowledgment seems absent;
- resents another person receiving praise;
- uses title or authority to protect standing;
- prioritizes visible work over necessary low-visibility work;
- exaggerates contribution when ownership is ambiguous;
- minimizes others’ contribution to preserve relative status;
- relies increasingly on approval to sustain effort.

## Safe conditional triggers

- contribution omitted from public credit;
- unclear ownership or role attribution;
- another person receiving praise for shared work;
- title loss, demotion or reduced visibility;
- public disagreement interpreted as status loss;
- repeated invisible or support work;
- comparison with a highly visible peer;
- leadership transition or fear of replacement;
- inconsistent recognition standards;
- praise-heavy environments that make approval feel necessary.

These are possible contexts, not deterministic causes.

## Protective factors

- clear ownership and credit rules before work begins;
- specific contribution-based acknowledgment;
- shared-credit norms for collaborative outcomes;
- transparent promotion, title and reward criteria;
- regular recognition of invisible and support work;
- rotating visible opportunities;
- direct clarification before assuming disrespect;
- outcome and learning goals alongside recognition goals;
- separating personal worth from visibility and rank;
- continuing meaningful effort when praise is delayed;
- recording contribution objectively where accountability matters;
- recognizing others as a leadership responsibility.

## Manifestation areas

### Work and study

May value visible ownership and accurate credit. Under pressure may prioritize high-visibility tasks, feel overlooked or contest attribution. Benefits from written ownership, contribution logs and shared-credit rules.

### Communication

May respond well to direct acknowledgment and respectful feedback. Under pressure may interpret disagreement or omission as disrespect. Benefits from separating content disagreement from status evaluation.

### Relationships and home

May appreciate appreciation for effort and responsibility. Under pressure may keep score or feel unseen. Benefits from explicit appreciation, reciprocal recognition and direct requests rather than assumptions.

### Leadership and teamwork

May be motivated by responsibility and visible contribution. Under pressure may centralize credit or underrecognize support work. Benefits from team-credit rituals, dissent tolerance and transparent attribution.

### Public roles and status

May notice titles, visibility and public evaluation. Under pressure may overinvest in rank or image. Benefits from contribution-based standards and stable identity outside status signals.
