## Review objective

Separate M1-R006 from already canonical Moolank 1 records and prevent authority language from becoming a duplicate of leadership, autonomy, recognition, dominance, coercion or personality diagnosis.

## Final construct definition

M1-R006 concerns how decision rights affecting a role, process or group are accepted, exercised, delegated, explained, limited and reviewed.

The app-facing label should be:

> **Authority and Decision Responsibility**

The internal research label may remain `Authority and Control Orientation`, but `control` should not be the primary user-facing term because it can imply domination or a fixed controlling personality.

## Approved traditional core

Moolank 1 tradition may associate this number with comfort in visible responsibility, management, decision-making and authority roles. In a balanced expression, authority is paired with accountability, clear role limits, consultation, meaningful delegation, transparent reasoning and respect for other people's legitimate agency. Under pressure, especially when decision rights, status or control feel threatened, the same orientation may become unnecessary centralization, micromanagement, rank-based decisions or resistance to oversight.

This is a traditional interpretation only. Scientific research does not establish that a birth number predicts authority orientation, dominance, obedience, reactance or management ability.

## Locked distinctions

### Authority versus leadership

Leadership is influence and coordination toward a shared outcome. Authority is a recognized decision right. A person may lead without authority or hold authority without leading well.

M1-R006 must not repeat broad leadership claims such as creating vision, motivating people or naturally taking charge. It may mention direction only when explaining the exercise of legitimate decision responsibility.

### Authority versus autonomy

Autonomy concerns agency over one's own choices. Authority concerns decisions that may affect a role, process or other people. Personal independence does not create authority over others.

M1-R006 must not frame resistance to supervision as its central strength. Resistance to perceived control remains primarily an autonomy/reactance boundary.

### Authority versus recognition and status

Recognition concerns acknowledgment, credit and public evaluation. Authority concerns legitimate decision rights and accountability. A title may increase visibility without granting unlimited authority, and authority does not prove worth or competence.

M1-R006 may include status threat only as a conditional trigger for misuse of authority, not as the core construct.

### Authority versus dominance

Dominance prioritizes rank and control over people. Responsible authority is bounded by purpose, role, consent, law, accountability and review.

Dominance is not an approved strength expression.

### Authority versus coercion

Coercion relies on threat, punishment or forced compliance. Legitimate authority may set justified limits, but must not be equated with unrestricted force or intimidation.

### Authority versus micromanagement

Micromanagement is excessive control over details that can reasonably be delegated. Responsible authority specifies outcomes, constraints, escalation rules and review points while preserving executional ownership.

### Authority versus entitlement

Entitlement assumes special rights without adequate role, agreement, contribution or accountability. Legitimate authority must be assigned, earned, legally defined or consented to within clear limits.

### Authority versus psychological reactance

Reactance is resistance to a perceived threat to freedom. It is a general psychological construct and is not a scientifically established Moolank 1 trait. A person may value legitimate authority while resisting illegitimate control.

## Approved balanced expressions

1. Accepts authority together with accountability.
2. Clarifies who decides what, within which limits and for which outcome.
3. Explains decision criteria when transparency is appropriate.
4. Consults people with relevant expertise or stake.
5. Delegates meaningful authority, not only tasks.
6. Creates escalation and review routes.
7. Revises decisions when evidence or conditions change.
8. Separates urgent command from routine collaboration.
9. Respects consent, law, policy and legitimate personal agency.
10. Uses rank to carry responsibility, not to end discussion.
11. Accepts oversight without treating review as automatic disrespect.
12. Takes responsibility for foreseeable consequences.

## Approved conditional shadows

These patterns are possibilities under pressure, not fixed traits:

1. Centralizes decisions beyond what the role requires.
2. Delegates work while retaining every meaningful choice.
3. Micromanages execution.
4. Treats disagreement as disloyalty.
5. Uses title or rank as proof of correctness.
6. Resists review, audit or oversight.
7. Overrules expertise without adequate evidence.
8. Interprets consultation as loss of control.
9. Creates avoidable power struggles.
10. Applies rules inconsistently to preserve personal influence.
11. Confuses responsibility for an outcome with ownership of other people's choices.
12. Reacts strongly when decision rights are unclear or reduced.

## Conditional triggers

- unclear decision rights;
- responsibility without adequate authority;
- authority without clear accountability;
- public challenge to a role or decision;
- sudden reduction of decision scope;
- disputed ownership;
- high-stakes deadlines;
- low trust;
- repeated bypassing of agreed processes;
- ineffective delegation;
- inconsistent oversight;
- fear of visible failure;
- status threat occurring together with responsibility pressure.

## Protective factors

- written decision-rights matrix;
- clear role limits;
- explicit accountability;
- consultation thresholds;
- meaningful delegation;
- review and appeal routes;
- evidence-based decision criteria;
- separation of urgent and routine authority;
- transparent conflict-of-interest rules;
- consent and agency safeguards;
- scheduled decision reviews;
- shared-credit norms;
- permission to revise without humiliation;
- leadership coaching or peer challenge in high-impact roles.

## App-safe reflection prompts

- Do I have legitimate authority for this decision, or only a strong preference?
- Who is affected and what consultation is proportionate?
- Have I delegated the outcome but kept all meaningful choices?
- Am I using rank to clarify accountability or to stop disagreement?
- What evidence would make me revise this decision?
- Is this truly urgent, or does consultation merely feel uncomfortable?
- Am I protecting the shared outcome or protecting my status?
- What review or appeal route exists if this decision is wrong?

## App-safe micro-actions

1. Before a high-impact decision, write: decision owner, consulted people, limits and review date.
2. Delegate one outcome together with its real decision space.
3. Ask one informed dissenter for the strongest counterargument.
4. Replace one rank-based statement with an evidence-based explanation.
5. Define what would trigger revision before implementation.
6. After a decision, record one benefit, one cost and one lesson.

## Prohibited claims

- Every Moolank 1 person wants to control others.
- Moolank 1 people are born rulers or superior decision-makers.
- Authority proves competence, intelligence or moral worth.
- Moolank 1 people cannot follow rules or work under supervision.
- Dominance, fear or humiliation are necessary for leadership.
- Micromanagement is responsible authority.
- Resistance to authority is always healthy autonomy.
- Birth number predicts obedience, dominance, reactance, management success, political power or criminal behaviour.
- Authority orientation indicates narcissism, a personality disorder, aggression or abuse.

## Overlap decision

- Remove generic `creates direction`, `takes charge`, `moves groups into action` language unless specifically tied to formal decision responsibility; these belong primarily to M1-R001.
- Remove generic `protects freedom`, `resists imposed control`, `prefers independent choice` language; these belong primarily to M1-R003.
- Remove generic `wants respect`, `seeks acknowledgment`, `cares about visibility` language; these belong primarily to M1-R005.
- Retain `status threat` only as one conditional trigger that may affect the use of authority.
- Retain `overcontrol` only when expressed through decision centralization, delegation failure, rank use or oversight resistance.

## Classification decision

Recommended classification:

`secondary-traditional-tendency-with-conditional-shadow`

Rationale:

- authority and management themes have moderate direct traditional support;
- precise responsible-authority formulation is primarily a safety and application framework;
- universal control-seeking or dominance is unsupported;
- scientific evidence linking authority orientation to Moolank 1 is not established.
